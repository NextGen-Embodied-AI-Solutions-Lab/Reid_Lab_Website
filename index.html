Autonomy, robotics, and AI for agriculture

About the lab

AI is moving from screens into the physical world. The Reid Lab works on making that transition succeed outdoors: on farms, on construction sites, and on the water, where machines face weather, variable terrain, and people working nearby.

Our research builds on Dr. Reid's four decades of work in machine-vision guidance and field robotics, in both academia and industry. We start from problems raised by growers, operators, and industry partners, test our methods on real machines in real conditions, and think early about safety, standards, and how research becomes products.

Prospective students

The Reid Lab builds AI and autonomy for machines that work outdoors, and tests it on real hardware: field robots, an autonomous boat, and tools that keep these machines safe around people. If that's the kind of work you want to do, we'd like to hear from you.

What we look for

  • Solid programming skills in Python or C++.
  • Interest in robotics, computer vision, or machine learning. Experience with ROS 2, sensors, or embedded hardware helps but isn't required.
  • Willingness to take work out of simulation and into the field, and to document it so the next student can build on it.

Ways to join

Ph.D.
Lead a research direction over several years, usually tied to one of the lab's current projects. Apply to the Computer Science or Agricultural and Biological Engineering Ph.D. program at Illinois and mention the Reid Lab in your application. Assistantships depend on available funding.
M.S.
Take ownership of a well-defined part of a project, for a thesis or for research credit.
Undergraduate
Help build and test systems alongside graduate students. Students in any year can reach out.

How to get in touch

Email Dr. Reid with:

  1. A subject line that starts with Prospective student and the degree you're seeking, for example "Prospective student: M.S."
  2. Your CV or résumé.
  3. A few sentences on which project interests you and what you'd bring to it.
Email Dr. Reid

Dr. Reid receives many of these emails and can't reply to every one, but you'll hear back if there's a good fit.
